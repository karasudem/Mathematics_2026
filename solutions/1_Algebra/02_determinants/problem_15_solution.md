Exercise 15 – Solution

Given:

det(A) = -2

det(B) = 3

We need to calculate:

det(B^(-1) A^T B)

Step 1: Apply the Product Property

The determinant of a product is equal to the product of the determinants.

det(B^(-1) A^T B) = det(B^(-1)) * det(A^T) * det(B)

Step 2: Apply the Inverse Property

The determinant of an inverse matrix is the reciprocal of the original determinant.

det(B^(-1)) = 1 / det(B)

det(B^(-1)) = 1 / 3

Step 3: Apply the Transpose Property

The determinant of a matrix is equal to the determinant of its transpose.

det(A^T) = det(A)

det(A^T) = -2

Step 4: Calculate the Result

Substitute the values:

det(B^(-1) A^T B) = (1 / 3) * (-2) * 3

The factors 1/3 and 3 cancel each other.

Therefore:

det(B^(-1) A^T B) = -2

Step 5: Explain Why the Factors Cancel

The determinant of B^(-1) is 1/3, while the determinant of B is 3.

Their product is:

(1 / 3) * 3 = 1

Therefore, the factors involving B cancel each other.

Only det(A^T) remains, which is equal to det(A).

Final Answer

det(B^(-1) A^T B) = -2

We used three properties:

1. Product property: det(ABC) = det(A) * det(B) * det(C).
2. Inverse property: det(B^(-1)) = 1 / det(B).
3. Transpose property: det(A^T) = det(A).
