Exercise 7 – Solution

Given:

det(A) = -4

We need to calculate:

det(2A), det(A^T), det(A^2), and det(-A).

Step 1: Calculate det(2A)

For a 3x3 matrix:

det(kA) = k^3 * det(A)

Therefore:

det(2A) = 2^3 * (-4)

det(2A) = 8 * (-4)

det(2A) = -32

Step 2: Calculate det(A^T)

The determinant of a matrix is equal to the determinant of its transpose.

det(A^T) = det(A)

det(A^T) = -4

Step 3: Calculate det(A^2)

The determinant of a product is the product of the determinants.

det(A^2) = det(A) * det(A)

det(A^2) = (-4) * (-4)

det(A^2) = 16

Step 4: Calculate det(-A)

For a 3x3 matrix:

det(-A) = (-1)^3 * det(A)

det(-A) = -1 * (-4)

det(-A) = 4

Final Answer

det(2A) = -32

det(A^T) = -4

det(A^2) = 16

det(-A) = 4

These results are obtained using determinant properties without calculating the matrix entries.
